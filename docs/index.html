Content Refresh Prioritization: A Transparent Baseline and Honest ML Evaluation

A decision-support workflow for ranking content pages for human refresh review using search visibility and freshness signals.

Prepared as a FlyRank ML Internship capstone project.

Abstract

This study asks which content pages should be prioritized for human review when editorial capacity for content refresh work is limited. The workflow uses anonymized page-level search and content signals and compares a transparent stale-and-visible baseline with a Random Forest classifier. On the client-grouped evaluation split, the baseline achieved a Precision@50 of 0.620, while the Random Forest achieved 0.560. A row-level random split produced a substantially higher model Precision@50 of 0.880, illustrating that validation design can materially change the measured estimate. The current evidence therefore supports the simpler baseline as the stronger decision-support approach on the evaluated grouped split, while the results remain observational and do not establish that refreshing a page causes traffic gains.

1. Introduction / Problem Statement

Content teams may have more pages available for review than they can realistically inspect or refresh in a limited period. The practical problem is therefore not simply to label pages, but to create a ranked review queue that helps editors decide where to look first.

The research question is: Which content pages should be prioritized for human review for a possible refresh, using page-level search visibility and freshness signals available at the decision time?

The intended decision is prioritization. A recommendation identifies a page that may deserve human inspection; it does not automatically authorize rewriting, publishing, deleting, redirecting, or otherwise changing the page.

The project began with a transparent rule-based baseline and then tested whether a learned model could provide better ranking quality under an honest client-grouped evaluation.

3. Methodology

Target definition

For evaluation, a declining-performance proxy was defined from the observed outcome window. A page was treated as declining when its later search-performance outcome indicated a decline relative to the relevant earlier period. This is a proxy for evaluation and is not treated as a direct measure of content quality or a causal outcome of refreshing a page.

Baseline

The baseline is intentionally simple. A page enters the strongest refresh-review category when:

  • it has at least 180 days since its last update, and
  • it has at least 500 impressions in the last 90 days.

Qualifying pages are ranked by impressions_90d. This produces a transparent rule that an editor can understand without inspecting model internals.

Learned model

A Random Forest classifier was evaluated using the six decision-time features listed above. Missing numeric values were handled with median imputation inside the modelling pipeline.

Validation design

The primary validation design is a client-grouped holdout. Entire clients are assigned to either training or test, preventing pages from the same client from appearing in both groups.

The grouped evaluation used 25 training clients and 7 test clients, with zero client overlap.

Leakage control

The workflow explicitly excluded label-derived information, future outcomes, trend-answer fields, and product decision flags from model inputs. A separate leakage audit found no forbidden fields in the final predictive feature set.

Primary metric

Precision@50 is the main evaluation metric because the operational output is a ranked review queue. The metric measures the fraction of the top 50 ranked pages that are actually labelled as declining under the evaluation proxy.

5. Limitations & Honest Framing

  • This analysis does not establish that refreshing a page causes traffic or ranking improvement.
  • The decline target is an observed proxy and may reflect seasonality, demand changes, tracking differences, or other external factors.
  • The Random Forest was evaluated on one client-grouped holdout, so the reported grouped result is not a guarantee of performance on every future client or period.
  • The row-level random split produced a much more optimistic score, demonstrating that validation design materially affects measured performance.
  • The baseline uses only staleness and recent visibility and does not capture all editorial, technical, business, or search-intent context.
  • Some feature values may be missing or unevenly available across pages.
  • The recommendation output is decision-support for human review, not an automatic instruction to change content.

These limitations are part of the result rather than an afterthought: the practical value of the workflow depends on keeping its claims narrower than the evidence permits.
