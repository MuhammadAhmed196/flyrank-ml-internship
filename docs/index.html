Content Refresh Prioritization: A Transparent Baseline and Honest ML Evaluation

A decision-support workflow for ranking content pages for human refresh review using search visibility and freshness signals.

Content Refresh Prioritization · Public-safe research artifact

1. Title + Abstract

This study asks which content pages should be prioritized for human review when editorial capacity for content refresh work is limited. The workflow uses anonymized page-level search and content signals and compares a transparent stale-and-visible baseline with a Random Forest classifier. On the client-grouped evaluation split, the baseline achieved a Precision@50 of 0.620, while the Random Forest achieved 0.560. A row-level random split produced a substantially higher Random Forest Precision@50 of 0.880, illustrating that validation design can materially change measured performance. The current evidence therefore supports the simpler baseline as the stronger decision-support approach on the evaluated grouped split, while the results remain observational and do not establish that refreshing a page causes traffic gains.

2. Introduction / Problem Statement

Content teams may have more pages available for review than they can realistically inspect or refresh in a limited period. The practical problem is therefore not simply to label pages, but to create a ranked review queue that helps editors decide where to look first.

The research question is: Which content pages should be prioritized for human review for a possible refresh, using page-level search visibility and freshness signals available at the decision time?

The intended decision is prioritization. A recommendation identifies a page that may deserve human inspection; it does not automatically authorize rewriting, publishing, deleting, redirecting, or otherwise changing the page.

The project therefore starts with a transparent rule-based baseline and evaluates whether a learned model improves the quality of the ranked review queue under an honest validation design.

4. Methodology

Target definition

A declining-performance proxy was used for evaluation. The target indicates whether later observed search performance declined relative to the earlier comparison window. It is an evaluation proxy rather than a direct measure of content quality or a causal outcome of refreshing a page.

Week-4 baseline

The transparent baseline identifies pages that are both stale and visible:

  • at least 180 days since the last update;
  • at least 500 impressions in the last 90 days.

Qualifying pages are ranked by impressions_90d.

Week-5 Random Forest

A Random Forest classifier was evaluated using the same six decision-time features. Missing numeric values were handled through median imputation within the modelling pipeline.

Validation

The primary evaluation uses a client-grouped holdout. Entire clients are assigned to either training or test, preventing pages from the same client from appearing in both groups.

The grouped evaluation contained 25 training clients and 7 test clients with zero client overlap.

Leakage control

The workflow explicitly excluded the target, future outcomes, trend-answer fields, and product-decision fields from predictive inputs. The dedicated leakage audit reported: Feature-list leakage check: PASS.

Primary metric

Precision@50 is the primary metric because the operational output is a ranked review queue. It measures the fraction of the top 50 ranked pages that match the declining-performance evaluation proxy.

6. Limitations & Honest Framing

  • The analysis does not establish that refreshing a page causes traffic or ranking improvement.
  • The declining target is an observed proxy and may reflect seasonality, demand changes, measurement differences, or other external factors.
  • The Random Forest was evaluated on one client-grouped holdout, so the result is not a guarantee of performance on every future client or time period.
  • The row-level random split produced a substantially more optimistic score, illustrating that validation design can materially affect measured performance.
  • The baseline uses only staleness and recent visibility and does not capture all business, editorial, technical, or search-intent context.
  • Some feature values may be missing or unevenly available.
  • The recommendation system is decision-support for human review, not an automatic instruction to modify content.
Safe interpretation: the current evidence supports the baseline as the stronger method on the evaluated client-grouped split. It does not show that the baseline will always outperform machine learning.

Human-review playbook

Before action

  • Confirm the page is still published.
  • Confirm the visibility signal is current.
  • Check that the page intent remains relevant.
  • Confirm there is an editorial reason for review.
  • Check for a recent update not yet reflected in the data.

No-go cases

  • Major technical issue.
  • Intentional seasonal or time-sensitive content.
  • Protected or sensitive content.
  • Recent update not yet represented in the data.
  • Insufficient evidence for a content change.

The recommendation must not automatically publish, rewrite, delete, redirect, or otherwise modify a page.
